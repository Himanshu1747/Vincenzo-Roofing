Local Expertise

Roofing Built for New Hampshire Weather.

A roof in Seabrook takes a different beating than one in Manchester. We build for salt air, coastal wind, and the freeze-thaw cycle that pulls New England roofs apart — because that’s what’s actually hitting your house.

Salt air near the coast

Within a few miles of the water, salt accelerates corrosion on fasteners, flashing, and drip edge. We spec stainless or coated components on Seacoast jobs instead of the standard galvanized most crews default to.

Wind exposure

Open coastal stretches see gusts that lift improperly nailed shingles. Correct nailing pattern, starter strip, and a shingle rated for the exposure matter far more here than the brand name on the bundle.

Ice dams and freeze-thaw

Meltwater refreezes at the cold eave and backs up under the shingles. Ice-and-water shield at the eaves and valleys, plus proper attic ventilation, is what stops it — not roof rakes in February.

Snow load and spring melt

Heavy wet snow followed by a fast thaw overwhelms undersized gutters and finds every weak flashing joint. We size drainage for the melt, not for an average rainfall.
