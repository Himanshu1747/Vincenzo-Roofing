How to Prevent Devastating Ice Dams on Your NH Roof This Winter

Ice dams are one of the leading causes of roof leaks and indoor water damage across Manchester and the Lakes Region. Learn how proper attic insulation and ventilation stop them before they start.

Every winter, homeowners from Manchester and Nashua up to the Lakes Region face one of New England’s most costly cold-weather threats: ice dams. These dense, heavy ridges of ice form along the edge of your roofline, blocking melting snow from draining into your gutters. As water pools behind the dam, it creeps upward under your asphalt shingles, leaking into your attic, ruining wall insulation, and causing severe interior drywall damage.

The key to stopping ice dams isn’t fighting the ice after it forms—it’s preventing the conditions that allow them to build up in the first place.

The Core Cause: Roof Temperature Imbalance

Ice dams are not simply created by cold temperatures and heavy snow. They are caused by a temperature mismatch on your roof deck. When heat escapes from your living space into the attic, it warms the top portion of the roof. Snow melts near the warm peak, trickles down, and refreezes when it hits the freezing eaves hanging over your exterior walls.

“If your roof is covered in snow while your neighbor’s roof is completely clear, your home is losing heat through the attic—and that heat loss is what fuels ice dams.”

4 Actionable Steps to Prevent Ice Dams Before Winter Hits

1. Air-Seal Attic Bypasses

Most heat escapes into the attic through hidden gaps, known as air leaks or bypasses. Common leak locations include:

  • Unsealed gaps around recessed ceiling lights (“can lights”)
  • Openings around chimney flues and plumbing stack vents
  • Uninsulated drop-down attic stairs and access hatches

Sealing these gaps with expandable foam or caulk is the single most effective way to keep warm air inside your living space.

2. Upgrade Attic Insulation to Northern Standards

In New Hampshire, current building standards recommend an insulation value between R-49 and R-60 for attics. Adequate insulation prevents conditioned heat from warming the roof sheath, keeping your roof deck at a uniform, sub-freezing temperature.

3. Maintain Balanced Soffit and Ridge Ventilation

Ventilation keeps cold air flowing under the roof deck to flush out any residual heat. Ensure that soffit vents at the eaves remain open and unobstructed by insulation baffles, and verify that your ridge vent at the roof peak is allowing warm air to exhaust properly.

4. Keep Gutters and Downspouts Cleaned Out

While clean gutters won’t stop an ice dam from forming, clogged gutters filled with autumn leaves hold standing water that freezes solid, creating an immediate anchor point for ice buildup when the first big snowstorm hits.

💡 Pro Tip for NH Homeowners

After a heavy New England snowstorm, use an extendable roof rake from the safety of the ground to clear the lower 3 to 6 feet of snow from your roof eaves. Eliminating the snow supply near the eaves stops meltwater from backing up into an ice dam.

Emergency Measures: What NOT to Do

If an ice dam forms on your home this winter, avoid making these critical mistakes that can permanently ruin your shingles:

  • Do NOT chip away with axes or hammers: Chopping at ice punctures shingles, destroys gutter hangers, and voids roofing warranties.
  • Do NOT scatter rock salt: Rock salt and standard ice melt degrade asphalt granules and rust aluminum flashing and gutter brackets.
  • DO call for professional steam removal: Professional roofing crews use specialized, low-pressure hot water steamers to safely melt through ice dams without compromising your roof deck.
