What To Do While You Wait For Us

If water is actively coming in, these six things matter more than anything else. They protect your family, limit the damage, and give your insurance claim the evidence it needs.

First

Keep everyone clear

Move people and pets away from the affected rooms. Sagging ceilings can come down without warning.

Second

Cut the power if needed

If water is anywhere near light fixtures, outlets, or the panel, shut off electricity to those rooms at the breaker.

Third

Photograph everything first

Before you move a single item or mop anything up, take photos and video. This is the evidence your claim rests on.

Fourth

Then contain the water

Move furniture and valuables out, put down buckets and towels, and lift rugs off wet flooring.

Fifth

Call us

We will give you an arrival window on the phone and get a tarp over the opening, usually the same day.

Sixth

Open your claim

Call your insurer to start the claim. Keep every receipt, including ours for the emergency tarping.

Please do not go up on the roof. Wet or storm damaged
roofs are how people get seriously hurt, and a fall will cost you far more than any repair. Let us assess it. If a limb is resting on the structure or you can see the roofline sagging, leave the house and call us from outside.

What We Handle

Storm Damage We Respond To

New Hampshire produces a specific set of roof emergencies. These are the ones our phone rings for.

01

Wind and nor’easter damage

Lifted or missing shingles, torn underlayment, and stripped ridge caps. Common along the Seacoast and on exposed hilltop properties.

02

Fallen trees and limbs

Impact damage ranging from punctured shingles to broken rafters. We assess the structure before anything else happens.

03

Hail damage

Bruised granules and cracked shingles that often look minor from the ground but shorten roof life significantly.

04

Ice dam leaks

Meltwater backing up under shingles at the eaves, usually showing first as stains on upstairs ceilings and walls.

05

Active interior leaks

Water coming through ceilings or running down walls. We stop the ingress first, then find and fix the source.

06

Flashing and vent failure

Storm forces open chimney flashing, vent boots, and skylight seals, creating a direct path for water.

07

Gutter and fascia damage

Ice and debris pull gutters away from the fascia, dumping water against the wall and into the foundation.

08

Commercial roof failures

Membrane tears, blown flashing, and blocked drains on flat roofs, handled with the same urgency.

Insurance Claims

Getting Your Claim Paid Properly

Most storm claims are won or lost on documentation. Here is how coverage generally works in New Hampshire, and how we support the process.

Usually covered
  • Wind damage, including lifted and missing shingles
  • Hail impact damage to the roof surface
  • Fallen trees and limbs, plus the resulting structural damage
  • Sudden water ingress and the interior damage it causes
  • Reasonable emergency mitigation such as tarping
  • Debris removal related to the covered event
Usually not covered
  • General wear and a roof that has reached its age
  • Damage from deferred maintenance or neglect
  • Pre existing leaks that were never repaired
  • Poor original installation by a previous contractor
  • Cosmetic marks with no effect on performance
  • Damage excluded specifically in your policy wording

What we do for your claim

We photograph the roof and interior before anything is moved, document the cause and the date of loss, prepare a written scope and estimate in the format adjusters expect, and attend the inspection with your adjuster so items are agreed on site rather than argued afterwards.

What we will never ask you to sign

We do not use assignment of benefits agreements. Signing one hands a contractor control of your claim and your settlement money, and it is a frequent source of disputes after big storms. You keep control of your claim. We provide the documentation and do the work, and your insurer pays you.

Local Expertise

The Storms That Damage New Hampshire Roofs

Knowing what a nor’easter does differently to a coastal roof than an inland one is what makes the difference between a fast diagnosis and a slow one.

Nor’easters on the Seacoast

Sustained wind driving rain sideways finds its way under shingle edges and behind siding in ways ordinary rainfall never does. Towns like Seabrook, Hampton, and Rye take the worst of it, and damage often shows up well away from the point of entry.

Ice dams in January and February

The most common winter emergency we attend. Warm attic air melts snow mid roof, it refreezes at the cold eave, and the backed up water lifts shingles and runs into the wall cavity. Insurers treat this differently depending on cause, which is why documentation matters.

Heavy wet snow and rapid thaw

A March snow that turns to rain adds enormous weight fast, overloading gutters and stressing older roof structures. Flat and low slope commercial roofs are especially vulnerable when drains are blocked.

Summer wind and fallen limbs

Thunderstorm downbursts bring limbs down onto roofs across the state. Impact damage often looks minor from the ground while the decking underneath is cracked, which is why we assess from the roof and the attic.
