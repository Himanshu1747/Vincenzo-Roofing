What We Do

An Honest Answer, In Writing

Plenty of companies offer a free roof inspection. The difference is what happens next, and whether the answer you get depends on what the inspector is hoping to sell you.

Our inspection produces a written condition report with photographs, covering what we found, what it means, and what if anything needs doing now. You keep that report whether or not you ever hire us. A meaningful share of the roofs we look at turn out to have years of life left, and we say so plainly.

We inspect from above and below. The roof surface tells you one story, and the attic tells you another. Moisture staining on the sheathing, compressed insulation, and blocked soffit vents explain problems that are invisible from a ladder, and they are the reason two identical roofs on the same street can age eight years apart.

When To Book

When You Should Have Your Roof Inspected

Most people call us after something has already gone wrong. These are the moments worth calling before that.

01

Buying or selling a home

Buyers find out what they are taking on. Sellers head off a late negotiation by putting documented condition on the table early.

02

After a storm

Wind and hail damage often looks like nothing from the ground while the shingle surface has already been compromised.

03

Before filing a claim

A documented report with photographs and a probable cause gives your adjuster evidence rather than an argument.

04

Ahead of winter

Autumn is the right time. Small problems found in October are repairs. The same problems found in February are emergencies.

05

Once the roof passes fifteen

Most asphalt roofs here reach the end of service life between twenty and twenty five years. Fifteen is when planning starts.

06

Any ceiling stain at all

Water travels along rafters before it drops, so the stain is rarely under the leak. Finding the real source needs a proper look.

07

Recurring ice dams

Ice damming is a ventilation and insulation problem showing up at the eaves. The attic tells us why it keeps happening.

08

Before solar or other work

Nobody wants to remove a new solar array to replace the roof underneath it. Check the remaining life first.

The Inspection

Our Twenty Point Roof and Attic Check

Every inspection covers all twenty points, whether you called about one stain on a ceiling or about the whole roof.

Points 1 to 10

The roof surface

  • Shingle condition curling, cracking, blistering, and lifted tabs
  • Granule loss surface wear and deposits collecting in gutters
  • Missing or damaged shingles and any exposed underlayment
  • Chimney flashing counter flashing, step flashing, and crown condition
  • Wall and skylight flashing at every roof to wall transition
  • Valleys wear, debris build up, and the quality of any past repair
  • Pipe boots and vents cracked collars and failed seals
  • Ridge and hip caps plus ridge vent condition and seating
  • Drip edge and eaves including any evidence of past ice damming
  • Previous repairs whether earlier work was done properly or patched

Points 11 to 20

Structure, drainage and attic

  • Decking condition soft spots, sag, and movement underfoot
  • Gutters and downspouts pitch, capacity, blockage, and discharge
  • Fascia and soffit rot, paint failure, and secure fixing
  • Penetrations and mounts satellite dishes, vents, and old fixings
  • Tree contact overhanging limbs and debris loading
  • Attic daylight check visible gaps at the sheathing and eaves
  • Moisture staining on rafters, sheathing, and insulation
  • Mold and mildew growth indicating trapped humidity
  • Insulation depth, coverage, and whether it blocks soffit intake
  • Ventilation balance intake against exhaust, the root of most problems

Local Expertise

What We Look For in New Hampshire

Roofs fail here in particular ways. Knowing the local patterns is what turns an inspection into a diagnosis.

Evidence of past ice damming

Staining at the eaves, lifted shingle edges, and water marks on the sheathing above the wall plate. This tells us the attic is losing heat, which is a fixable cause rather than a roofing symptom.

Salt corrosion on the Seacoast

In Seabrook, Hampton, and Rye we check fasteners, flashing, and drip edge closely. Coastal roofs frequently fail at the metal long before the shingles themselves are worn out.

Wind lift patterns

Nor’easters lift shingles along specific edges and rake lines. The pattern tells us whether the nailing was correct originally, which matters a great deal for an insurance claim.

Ventilation balance

The most common underlying issue we find, and the most commonly missed. Ridge vent with blocked or absent soffit intake cooks shingles in summer and drives ice dams in winter.
