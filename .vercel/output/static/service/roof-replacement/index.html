Repair or Replace?

Signs It’s Time to Replace Your Roof

One or two of these usually means a repair will do. Several at once, or any of them on a roof past twenty years, and replacement is almost always the better spend.

01

Curling or cracked shingles

Edges lifting or corners cupping means the shingle has lost its flexibility and no longer seals against wind-driven rain.

02

Granules in your gutters

Those dark sandy deposits are the shingle’s protective layer washing away. Once it is gone, UV breaks down what is left quickly.

03

Leaks in more than one place

A single leak is a repair. Water showing up in separate areas usually means the system has failed, not one flashing detail.

04

A sagging or spongy deck

Dips along the roofline mean moisture has reached the sheathing. This one does not wait — the damage spreads under the surface.

05

Daylight through the attic

If you can see light between the boards from inside, water and cold air are getting in the same way.

06

Twenty years or older

Most asphalt roofs in New Hampshire reach the end of their service life somewhere between twenty and twenty-five years.

Your Options

Choosing Your Roofing Material

Two materials cover the vast majority of New Hampshire replacements. The right answer depends on how long you plan to stay, your roof’s pitch, and how exposed the house is.

Architectural asphalt shingles

The default for most homes, and for good reason: strong value, a wide colour range, and a 25–30 year service life when installed correctly. Near the coast or on exposed hillsides we specify a higher wind rating rather than the builder-grade product most quotes assume.

More on asphalt shingle roofing →

Standing-seam metal

Costs more up front and pays it back over forty-plus years. Metal sheds snow before it can build into an ice dam and holds up far better against coastal salt air. Worth serious consideration if you are staying in the house long term.

More on metal roofing →

 

A note on warranties

Manufacturer warranties cover defects in the material itself. They do not cover installation errors — and an improper install can void them entirely. That is why our 25-year workmanship warranty is separate and written down: between the two, you are covered on both sides.

Straight Pricing

How Much Does a Roof Replacement Cost in New Hampshire?

$8,000 – $22,000

That is the honest range for most New Hampshire homes. A small ranch with a simple gable roof sits near the bottom; a large colonial with multiple dormers, steep pitch, and metal roofing sits at the top. We would rather you see the range now than find it out after an hour-long sales visit.

  • Size and pitch — square footage plus how steep the roof is to work on safely
  • Material choice — asphalt vs. metal is the single biggest swing
  • Existing layers — two or three old layers means more tear-off and disposal
  • Deck condition — rotted sheathing found during tear-off is replaced at cost
  • Complexity — dormers, valleys, skylights, and chimneys all add flashing work
  • Access — tight lots and steep driveways affect staging and cleanup time

Local Expertise

Built for New Hampshire Weather

A roof in Seabrook takes a different beating than one in Manchester. We build for what is actually hitting your house.

 

Salt air on the Seacoast

Within a few miles of the water, salt corrodes fasteners, flashing, and drip edge far faster than inland. On coastal jobs we specify corrosion-resistant components instead of the standard galvanised most crews default to.

 

Coastal wind exposure

Open stretches near the shore see gusts that lift improperly nailed shingles. Correct nailing pattern, starter strip, and a shingle rated for the exposure matter far more here than the brand on the bundle.

 

Ice dams and freeze-thaw

Meltwater refreezes at the cold eave and backs up under the shingles. Ice-and-water shield at eaves and valleys, plus balanced attic ventilation, is what actually stops it.

 

Snow load and spring melt

Heavy wet snow followed by a fast thaw finds every weak flashing joint and overwhelms undersized drainage. We size gutters for the melt, not for average rainfall.
