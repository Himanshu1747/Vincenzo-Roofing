The Material

The Workhorse of New Hampshire Roofs

Roughly four out of five homes in this state wear asphalt shingles. They are affordable, they come in every colour a neighbourhood association could ask for, and installed properly they will see you through two decades of New England weather.

Where homeowners get caught out is assuming all asphalt is the same. There is a meaningful gap between a builder-grade three-tab shingle and a laminated architectural shingle rated for 150 mph winds — in appearance, in lifespan, and in how it behaves during a February nor’easter.

The other half of the equation is installation. Nailing pattern, starter strip, ice-and-water shield placement, and attic ventilation determine whether a shingle reaches its rated life or falls well short of it. Most roofs that fail early were not defective — they were fitted badly.

This page covers the material itself: grades, wind ratings, lifespan, and pricing. If you want the process of getting a roof replaced, that is on our roof replacement page

Shingle Grades

Three Grades, Three Very Different Roofs

Every asphalt quote you receive will sit in one of these categories. Knowing which is how you compare two numbers fairly.

Budget

3-tab shingles

Single flat layer with cut-out tabs

  • Lowest cost per square of any shingle
  • Flat, uniform appearance with no shadow line
  • Lighter weight and lower wind resistance
  • Best suited to sheds, garages, and rental turnovers
Service life in NH: 15–20 years

Recommended

Architectural shingles

Multiple laminated layers, dimensional profile

  • Thicker and heavier — significantly better wind performance
  • Shadowed, textured look that suits most NH homes
  • Available in wind ratings up to 150 mph
  • The standard choice on the large majority of our jobs
Service life in NH: 20–25 years

Premium

Designer shingles

Heavyweight laminated, slate or shake profiles

  • Mimics slate or cedar shake at a fraction of the weight
  • Heaviest asphalt product with the longest rated life
  • Suits historic homes and high-value properties
  • Approaches metal on cost — worth comparing both
Service life in NH: 25–30 years

Wind Performance

What Wind Rating Do You Actually Need?

Shingles are tested to ASTM standards and sold in wind classes. On the Seacoast this matters far more than it does inland, and it is the spec most quotes leave out entirely.

Class D

90 mph

Basic rating found on most three-tab products. We do not specify this for primary residences anywhere in New Hampshire.

Class G

120 mph

Solid choice for sheltered inland homes. Standard on most quality architectural shingle lines.

Class H — our Seacoast spec

150 mph

What we recommend for coastal and exposed hilltop sites. The upgrade cost is small relative to replacing wind-lifted sections.

The rating only counts if the install matches it

A 150 mph shingle nailed in the wrong place performs like a 90 mph shingle, and the manufacturer’s wind warranty will not cover it. High-wind ratings require a specific nail count, precise placement in the nailing zone, and a proper starter strip along both eaves and rakes. This is the single most common corner cut in roofing, and it is invisible once the roof is finished.

Lifespan

What Decides How Long Your Shingles Last

Two identical roofs on the same street can differ by eight years. These are the reasons why.

01

Attic ventilation

The biggest single factor, and the most overlooked. Trapped heat cooks shingles from underneath and can strip years off a roof’s life.

02

Nailing accuracy

Nails placed too high, too low, overdriven, or too few. Each one is a future lifted tab and a voided wind warranty.

03

Sun exposure

South and west-facing slopes age faster. It is normal to see one side of a roof wear noticeably ahead of the other.

04

Ice damming

Water backing up under shingles at the eaves lifts and loosens them. Ice-and-water shield is the defence, ventilation is the cure.

05

Overhanging trees

Shade holds moisture and encourages moss and algae; falling limbs bruise granules. Trimming back pays for itself.

06

Coastal salt air

Near the water, salt corrodes fasteners and flashing well before the shingles themselves are worn out.

Straight Pricing

How Much Does an Asphalt Shingle Roof Cost?

$8,000 – $22,000

That is the honest range for most New Hampshire homes. A modest ranch with a simple gable roof in three-tab sits near the bottom; a large colonial in designer shingles with multiple dormers sits at the top. You get an itemized written estimate after an on-site inspection.

  • Roof size and pitch — square footage plus how steep it is to work safely
  • Shingle grade — three-tab, architectural, or designer
  • Wind rating — Class G versus Class H on exposed sites
  • Existing layers — two or three old layers means more tear-off
  • Deck condition — rotted sheathing found during tear-off
  • Complexity — dormers, valleys, skylights, and chimneys

Worth Considering

Should You Look at Metal Instead?

Asphalt is usually right when…

You are working to a defined budget, you may sell within ten to fifteen years, your roof has a complex shape with many angles, or you want the widest possible choice of colour and profile. For most New Hampshire homes, architectural asphalt is the sensible answer.

Metal is worth pricing when…

You intend to stay long term, the property is coastal or badly exposed, ice dams have been a recurring problem, or you are already considering designer shingles — at that price point metal becomes genuinely competitive over the life of the roof.

Local Expertise

Specifying Asphalt for New Hampshire

The same shingle behaves differently in Seabrook than in Keene. These are the details we adjust by location.

Higher wind rating near the coast

Seacoast and exposed hilltop properties get Class H shingles rated to 150 mph as standard. Inland and sheltered sites are generally well served by Class G.

Ice-and-water shield placement

Code sets a minimum at the eaves. On low-pitch roofs and known ice dam trouble spots we run it further up the slope and through every valley.

Corrosion-resistant fasteners

Near the water, standard nails and flashing corrode years before the shingles wear out. Coastal jobs get coated or stainless components as standard.

Ventilation sized to the attic

Ridge vent alone is not a ventilation system. Intake at the soffit has to balance the exhaust, or you get heat build-up in summer and ice dams in winter.
