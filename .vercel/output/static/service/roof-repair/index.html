What We Do

Repairs That Fix the Cause, Not the Symptom

Water rarely enters a roof where the stain appears on your ceiling. It travels along rafters and sheathing before it drops, sometimes several feet from the actual failure.

That is why patching the visible spot so often fails. We trace the leak back to its source first — the lifted flashing, the split boot, the nail that backed out — and repair that. It takes longer than slapping sealant on a ceiling stain, and it is the reason our repairs hold.

Vincenzo’s Roofing repairs residential and commercial roofs across New Hampshire from our base in Seabrook. Every repair is quoted in writing before we start, and warrantied in writing when we finish.

What We Fix

Common Roof Problems We Repair

Most of what we get called out for falls into one of these. If yours is not listed, it is still worth a call — we have seen a lot of roofs.

01

Roof leaks

Ceiling stains, damp attic insulation, or drips during heavy rain. We trace it back to the entry point rather than sealing where it shows.

02

Failed flashing

Chimneys, skylights, walls, and valleys. Flashing is where most roofs actually fail, and it is the most commonly botched repair.

03

Missing or blown-off shingles

Wind damage leaves the underlayment exposed. Left alone through one New Hampshire winter it becomes a much larger job.

04

Cracked pipe boots

The rubber collar around plumbing vents dries and splits after about a decade. A cheap part, and a very common leak source.

05

Ice dam damage

Meltwater backing up under shingles lifts and loosens them. We repair the damage and address the ventilation causing it.

06

Storm and impact damage

Fallen limbs, hail bruising, and wind-lifted sections — documented properly if you are filing an insurance claim.

07

Sagging or rotted decking

Soft spots underfoot mean water has reached the sheathing. We cut out the damaged section and rebuild it properly.

08

Ridge vent and ventilation issues

Poor airflow cooks shingles from beneath and drives ice dams in winter. Often the real cause behind repeat problems.

Honest Advice

Should You Repair or Replace?

We will tell you when a repair is throwing good money after bad — and equally, when your roof has years left and a salesman is overselling you.

A repair usually makes sense when…
  • The roof is under fifteen years old
  • Damage is isolated to one area
  • There is a single, traceable leak
  • Shingles are otherwise flat and intact
  • The problem is flashing, not the field of the roof
  • Storm damage hit one section only
Replacement is the better spend when…
  • The roof is past twenty years
  • Leaks are appearing in several separate places
  • Granules are filling the gutters
  • Shingles are curling or cracking across the roof
  • The deck is sagging or spongy underfoot
  • You have already repaired it two or three times

Local Expertise

Why New Hampshire Roofs Fail

The repairs we get called out for here are not the ones a roofer in a milder climate sees. Knowing the local failure patterns is half of finding the leak.

Ice dams at the eaves

Warm attic air melts snow mid-roof; it refreezes at the cold eave and backs water up under the shingles. The repair is only half the job — the ventilation causing it needs addressing too.

Salt corrosion on the Seacoast

Near the water, salt eats through fasteners, flashing, and drip edge years earlier than inland. We see failures in Seabrook and Hampton that simply do not happen in Concord.

Wind-lifted shingles

Exposed coastal and hilltop properties lose shingles to gusts, especially where the original nailing pattern was rushed. Repairs here need the correct fastener placement, not just replacement tabs.

Freeze-thaw on flashing

Repeated expansion and contraction works sealant loose around chimneys and skylights. It is the most common leak we trace in older New Hampshire homes.
