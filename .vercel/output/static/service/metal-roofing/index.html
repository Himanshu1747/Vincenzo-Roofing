A Roof That Outlives Two Asphalt Ones

Metal costs more up front. That is the honest starting point, and it is why most homeowners rule it out before they have done the arithmetic.

A metal roof in New Hampshire typically lasts forty years or more. Architectural asphalt reaches the end of its service life somewhere around twenty-five. If you are staying in the house long term, you are choosing between buying one metal roof or buying two asphalt roofs — and the second one will cost considerably more than today’s price.

Beyond longevity, metal earns its place here for a specific local reason: it sheds snow. Smooth standing-seam panels let accumulation slide off before it can melt, run to the cold eave, and refreeze into the ice dams that cause so much winter damage across the state.

Metal is also unforgiving of poor installation. Panel expansion, concealed clip placement, and flashing detail all have to be right, which is why our metal work is handled by crews trained specifically on these systems rather than shingle crews doing something unfamiliar.

Systems We Install

Types of Metal Roofing

Not all metal roofs are the same product. The panel profile matters as much as the material.

Recommended

Standing seam

Vertical panels joined by raised, concealed-fastener seams

  • No exposed fasteners to loosen or leak over time
  • Panels float on clips, allowing thermal expansion
  • Smoothest profile — best snow shedding of any system
  • Clean, architectural appearance on modern and traditional homes
Service life: 40–70 years

Traditional look

Metal shingles

Stamped panels that mimic slate, shake, or shingle profiles

  • Metal durability with a conventional roof appearance
  • Suits historic homes and neighbourhood design guidelines
  • Lighter than the slate or tile it imitates
  • Works well on complex rooflines with many angles
Service life: 40–50 years

Budget option

Exposed fastener panels

Corrugated or ribbed panels screwed directly through the face

  • Lowest installed cost of the metal options
  • Well suited to barns, garages, and outbuildings
  • Faster installation on simple, open roof shapes
  • Gaskets need periodic checking as they age
Service life: 25–40 years

What Metal Does Well in New Hampshire

01

Sheds snow before ice dams form

Smooth panels release accumulation rather than letting it melt, run to the cold eave, and refreeze under the roof edge.

02

Holds up to coastal salt air

With a coastal-grade coating system, metal resists the salt corrosion that shortens roof life across the Seacoast.

03

Very high wind resistance

Concealed clips lock panels down along their full length, with no individual tabs for gusts to lift and peel back.

04

Reflects summer heat

Reflective finishes cut attic heat gain, which lowers cooling load and eases the strain on upper-floor comfort.

05

Non-combustible

Metal will not ignite from embers, and some insurers offer a premium reduction for a metal roof — worth asking yours.

06

Almost no maintenance

Beyond an occasional fastener and sealant check at penetrations, a standing-seam roof asks very little of you.

Straight Answers

Four Things People Get Wrong About Metal Roofs

These come up on nearly every estimate. Here is the accurate answer to each.

The concern

“Metal roofs are loud in the rain.”

The reality

The noise people remember is bare metal over an open barn. A residential metal roof sits on solid decking with underlayment, above insulated attic space. Rain sound is comparable to asphalt, and most homeowners notice no difference at all.

The concern

“Metal attracts lightning.”

The reality

It does not. Metal neither attracts lightning nor makes a strike more likely. If lightning does hit, metal is the safer material — it is non-combustible and disperses the energy rather than igniting.

The concern

“It will dent in a hailstorm.”

The reality

Standing-seam panels in a suitable gauge handle typical New Hampshire hail without functional damage. Very large hail can leave cosmetic dimples on lighter gauges, but watertightness is unaffected. Heavier gauge and textured finishes reduce visible marking.

The concern

“It will rust out.”

The reality

Modern panels are galvanised or Galvalume-coated and finished with a baked paint system. Rust is a problem of bare or damaged steel. On the Seacoast we specify coastal-grade coatings and fasteners for exactly this reason.

Local Expertise

Specifying Metal for New Hampshire

The same panel performs very differently in Seabrook than it does in Keene. These are the details that decide whether a metal roof reaches forty years or thirty.

Coastal coating systems

Within a few miles of the water, standard finishes and fasteners corrode early. On Seacoast homes we specify coastal-grade coatings and stainless or coated fasteners as standard, not as an upsell.

Snow guards, placed deliberately

Metal sheds snow — which is the point, and also the hazard. Guards go above entrances, walkways, driveways, and anywhere a sudden release could damage landscaping or a parked car.

Thermal movement

Panels expand and contract measurably between a February night and an August afternoon. Concealed clips let them move; fastening them rigidly is how you get oil-canning, stressed seams, and eventual leaks.

Ventilation still matters

Metal reduces ice dams but does not replace a balanced attic. Ridge and intake venting stay part of the specification, because trapped warm air causes problems under any roofing material.
