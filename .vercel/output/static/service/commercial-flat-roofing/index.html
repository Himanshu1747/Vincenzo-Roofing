Systems We Install

Choosing the Right Membrane

Three systems cover almost every commercial roof in New Hampshire. The right one depends on your building’s cooling load, foot traffic, budget horizon, and what is up there already.

Most popular

TPO

White thermoplastic membrane with heat-welded seams

  • Reflects sunlight and lowers summer cooling costs
  • Heat-welded seams — no adhesive joints to fail
  • Strong resistance to punctures and chemical exposure
  • Best value for buildings with heavy air conditioning demand
Service life: 20–30 years

Best value

EPDM

Black synthetic rubber membrane, fully adhered or ballasted

  • Handles New Hampshire freeze-thaw cycles exceptionally well
  • Lower installed cost than TPO on most buildings
  • Simple to repair and patch over its service life
  • Dark surface helps shed snow load in winter
Service life: 20–30 years

Heavy duty

Modified bitumen

Multi-ply asphalt system, torch or cold-adhesive applied

  • Multiple layers give redundancy against punctures
  • Handles rooftop foot traffic and service access well
  • Good choice where equipment is regularly serviced
  • Proven performance on older existing substrates
Service life: 15–25 years

Not sure which applies to your building?

Assessment

Signs Your Flat Roof Needs Attention

Flat roofs rarely fail suddenly. They give warning for months, usually somewhere nobody is looking.

01

Water ponding after rain

Standing water more than 48 hours after rainfall accelerates membrane breakdown and adds serious weight to the structure.

02

Seams lifting or separating

Open seams are the most common entry point on any membrane roof, and the easiest failure to catch early.

03

Blisters and bubbling

Trapped moisture or air beneath the membrane. Left alone, blisters rupture and open a direct path for water.

04

Interior ceiling stains

By the time a tenant reports a stain, water has usually been travelling through the insulation for some time.

05

Failing flashing at penetrations

HVAC curbs, vents, and drains are where most commercial leaks begin — anywhere the membrane has been cut.

06

Rising energy costs

Wet insulation loses most of its R-value. An unexplained jump in heating or cooling spend often starts at the roof.

Local Expertise

What New Hampshire Does to a Flat Roof

Low-slope roofs face problems here that simply do not exist in milder climates. Specifying for them is the difference between twenty years and twelve.

Snow load and drift

Flat roofs hold snow rather than shedding it, and drifting piles it against parapets and mechanical units. Structural capacity and drainage both have to account for a heavy February, not an average one.

Freeze-thaw on seams

Repeated expansion and contraction works at every seam and flashing joint. Heat-welded TPO seams and properly detailed EPDM terminations hold up far better than adhesive-only assemblies.

Ponding and spring melt

A fast thaw dumps weeks of accumulated snow into the drainage system at once. Undersized or blocked drains turn that into standing water — which is how most flat roof failures actually begin.

Coastal salt exposure

On Seacoast properties, salt corrodes fasteners, edge metal, and rooftop equipment supports years ahead of schedule. We specify corrosion-resistant components on coastal commercial work as standard.
